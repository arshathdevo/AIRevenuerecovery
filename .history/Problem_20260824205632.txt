: simple terms
Imagine a user has a ₹999/month subscription for a learning app, SaaS tool, gym, or streaming service. Their payment fails because:

Their card expired or was replaced

They temporarily have insufficient balance

The bank declines a valid transaction

A network/payment-gateway error occurs

Their UPI/autopay mandate fails

The business retries at the wrong time or never contacts them

The customer may still want the service—but the business marks them as “churned” and loses their future revenue. This is called involuntary churn: a customer is lost due to payment failure, not an intentional cancellation. In subscription businesses, involuntary churn is often estimated at 20–40% of total churn.

Why current methods fail
Most small and mid-sized businesses handle recovery poorly:

They use the same retry rule for every failed payment.

They send generic reminders such as “Your payment failed.”

They do not distinguish a temporary failure from a permanently invalid card.

They fail to prioritize high-value customers or customers most likely to pay after a reminder.

Finance, customer support, and payment data live in separate tools.

They cannot easily see how much revenue is at risk, recovered, or permanently lost.

For example, retrying a payment immediately after an “insufficient funds” decline may fail again; retrying when the customer is more likely to have funds, or sending a simple payment-update link, could recover that revenue. Permanent issues such as a stolen, closed, or invalid card should not be retried repeatedly.
